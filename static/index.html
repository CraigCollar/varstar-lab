V0756 CrA · HADS(B)

How a photograph becomes a distance

Your target, V0756 CrA, is a high-amplitude δ Scuti star (type HADS(B)). That classification is the whole reason a distance is reachable: δ Scuti stars obey a period–luminosity relation, so measuring how fast the star pulses tells you how intrinsically bright it is. Compare that with how bright it looks, and the difference is distance. It is the same logic Henrietta Leavitt used on Cepheids, one rung down the ladder.

1. aperture photometry on every frame → flux(t) for the target and comparison stars
2. Δm = −2.5 log₁₀(F_target / ΣF_comp) → a light curve immune to cloud and airmass
3. Lomb–Scargle + phase dispersion minimisation → the pulsation period P
4. M_V = −2.94 log₁₀P − 1.34 → absolute magnitude (Ziaali et al. 2019)
5. d = 10^((⟨V⟩ − M_V − A_V)/5 + 1) parsecs → the distance

Two things the app will nag you about, because they are where this measurement usually goes wrong. First, a distance needs an apparent magnitude, not just a differential one — so you must supply catalog magnitudes for at least one comparison star. Second, one short night is not enough: if a continuous run does not cover a full pulsation cycle, the period picks up a 1 cycle/day ambiguity that no amount of extra short nights will fix.
